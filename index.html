Snail Sign was built by a bootstrapped founder. When you're building something from nothing, every dollar has a job. But I wasn't looking for the cheapest solution. I was asking a different question: why pay for enterprise complexity you'll never use?

Most signing tools are priced for procurement departments. They assume a legal team, a rollout plan and a seat count. A founder signing a contractor agreement needs none of that. They need the document to be signed, delivered, and provable later.

So Snail Sign does that, and stops. One price. No tiers. The document is prepared in your own browser, and every completed agreement carries a certificate you can check.

Why Snail Sign

Snails are slow, and they are careful, and they leave a trail behind them showing exactly where they have been. That is what an agreement should do.

Speed is not the point of signing a contract. Being able to prove what was agreed, by whom, and when, is the point. Snail Sign keeps that trail: who signed, at what time, and a SHA-256 fingerprint of the finished document so any later alteration shows up.

The rocket is there because none of that has to be slow to use.
