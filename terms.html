4. Electronic Signatures: Scope and Limits

We do not warrant that a document prepared, signed, or delivered using Snail Sign is legally binding, admissible, or enforceable in your jurisdiction or for your particular purpose.

Electronic signature law varies by country, by state, and by document type. Some documents, including wills and testamentary instruments, certain property transfers, certain family law and court documents, certain notices required by statute, and others depending on where you are, may require a handwritten signature, a witness, a notary, an apostille, or a qualified electronic signature issued by an accredited trust service provider. If the document matters, obtain legal advice about whether this tool is appropriate before you use it.

4.1 What Snail Sign does

  • Lets you place fields on a PDF or Word document, complete them, and apply a drawn, typed, or uploaded signature image;
  • Produces a flattened PDF containing the signatures that were applied, together with a SHA-256 fingerprint of the finished file;
  • Optionally routes the document by email, in sequence, to up to four signers, and returns the finished document to the sender and the signers.

4.2 What Snail Sign does not do

  • It does not verify a signer's identity. The only identifier is the email address you supply, and possession of an email address does not prove identity;
  • It does not perform knowledge-based authentication, government ID verification, one-time passcode challenges, or any other identity assurance step;
  • It does not issue certificate-based or qualified digital signatures, and it is not a qualified or accredited trust service provider under eIDAS or any equivalent regime;
  • It does not act as a notary, witness, or attesting authority;
  • It does not maintain a certificate of completion, a tamper-evident audit trail, or a long-term evidential record of who did what and when;
  • It does not review, approve, or advise on the content, legality, or adequacy of any document.

4.3 Your determination

You are solely responsible for determining whether an electronic signature is legally sufficient for your document, whether any additional formality is required, and whether the evidence Snail Sign produces is adequate for your needs. If you require an audit trail, identity verification, or a qualified electronic signature, you should use a service that provides them.
